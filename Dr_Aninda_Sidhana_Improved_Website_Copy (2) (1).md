## About

### Clinical knowledge. Human understanding. Responsibility beyond the clinic.

I am Dr. Aninda Sidhana, a psychiatrist with an MD in Psychiatry and a Fellowship in Psychosexual Medicine. My clinical interests include women’s mental health, sexuality and relationships, trauma, gender-based violence, and the social conditions that influence psychological wellbeing.

My training gave me a language for distress. Life taught me how much can remain unspoken.

That understanding informs my work, but so does a clear professional responsibility: to listen carefully, use evidence thoughtfully and recognise the person beyond the diagnosis.

Alongside clinical work, I contribute to public education, editorial conversations and institutional advocacy. I serve as National President of WICCI’s Mental Health & Psychosocial Wellness Council, Convener of the Indian Psychiatric Society LGBTQ Task Force, and Assistant Editor of the Indian Journal of Psychological Medicine.

I founded The Dignity Dialogues™ and co-host The Listening Commons with Scott Douglas Jacobsen.

Across these spaces, I return to the same concern: how can care reach the environments in which people actually live?

## Speaking and advisory

I welcome paid speaking and advisory inquiries from organisations seeking a clinically informed, humane approach to mental health.

Engagements can be shaped around leadership conversations, professional education, workplace wellbeing, media representation and responsible technology.

## WICCI Minds

### Mental health as a shared public responsibility.

Through WICCI’s Mental Health & Psychosocial Wellness Council, I work with colleagues across clinical care, research, advocacy, media and public life.

The council’s agenda connects mental health with the systems and conversations that shape everyday wellbeing. Its public record includes responsible reporting, suicide-prevention communication, women’s wellbeing, inclusion, cinema and dialogue on peace and dignity.

### Responsible reporting and storytelling
Council recommendations seek more accurate and sensitive treatment of suicide and mental illness, with attention to privacy, context and routes to support. Representations and the September addendum are submissions for institutional consideration; they are not described as adopted policy.

### Public mental-health education
Council communications bring attention to distress, help-seeking and community understanding. Documented themes include youth and school mental health, support across the lifespan and the pressures that often remain invisible.

### Women’s wellbeing and emotional labour
The council’s conversations address the psychological weight of expectations, caregiving and unequal emotional responsibility.

### Culture, peace and belonging
Public posts connect storytelling, women’s participation in peacebuilding, ageing, memory and dignity. No Peace Without Women and Cinema & the Mind form part of this wider dialogue.
